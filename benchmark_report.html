Which settings work best? A test of the parallel finder

This page tests the five settings at the top of matcher.py. For each setting, several values were tried, and for each value we measured how well the program finds parallels.

The two tests

1. The practice test. We hid passages copied from one Gospel inside a jumbled-up text. Some are exact copies, others have 10–40% of their words changed. Because we hid them ourselves, we know every right answer, so we can grade the program exactly.

What the scores mean

Imagine looking for 100 coins hidden on a beach with a metal detector.

Precision (correct): of everything you dug up, how much was really a coin and not a bottle cap? High precision = few false alarms.
Recall (found): of the 100 hidden coins, how many did you find? High recall = few missed.
Overall (F1): one grade that combines the two. It is only high when both are high, so this is the main score to look at.
Boundary fit: when a passage is found, how exactly its start and end are right (100% = exactly right).

How to read the charts

Each setting is changed on its own while the others stay as they are. The thin vertical line marked “current” is the value used now. Higher lines are better. Move the mouse over a chart to see the exact numbers, or click “Show all the numbers” under it. Differences of 1–2 points are too small to matter: they can change by chance.
